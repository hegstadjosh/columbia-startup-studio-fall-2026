# Usability synthesis: Gable

**Sessions run:** 3 (Nov 6, [Nov ?], Nov 11), on the staging MVP, over Zoom. Each of us ran and took notes for our own session.
**Participants who reached the aha moment (a short list of sublets that fit what they asked for):** 3 of 3.
Notes for each session are in `sessions/`.

## Patterns across sessions

| Pattern | Sessions |
|---|---|
| Tried "Sign in" before creating an account | 3 of 3 |
| Said there aren't enough listings, or wanted more options | 3 of 3 |
| Missed the chat search at first (scrolled or filtered by eye) | 2 of 3 (the third typed into it at once and loved it) |
| Messages unclear: "looks like WhatsApp", who am I messaging? | 2 of 3 (session 1 didn't reach Messages) |
| Travel-time lines on cards confused them ("Columbus Circle", "20 min to Times Square") | 2 of 3 |

## What surprised us

- The chat search is the aha moment, and it worked every time someone used it ("wowwwww this is great!!!!", "Saves me going through everything"). The problem isn't the search. It's that people don't always see it.
- Nobody got stuck on the actual search or saving a place. The friction is around the edges: sign-up, labels and Messages.
- Session 1's tester didn't realize the listings come from Columbia students, even though that's our main trust promise.

## What we're changing and why

> **What we observed:** All 3 testers clicked "Sign in" first and got an error or a pause before finding sign-up. Session 1's login "didn't work" until she realized she had no account. Session 2's login was "wrong." Session 3 said "Oh, wait, I don't have an account yet."
>
> **What we're changing:** Make "Sign up" the primary button (filled) in the header and on the sign-in page, and "Log in" a plain text link.
>
> **Why:** It cost every tester time on the first thing they tried, before they reached the product.

> **What we observed:** 2 of 3 testers didn't use the chat search at first. One scrolled the listings before noticing it, and one filtered by eye and never used the chat on the left. The tester who typed into it right away reached the aha moment fastest. Session 1's tester also clicked "Summer" in the header and left the search.
>
> **What we're changing:** Make the first page more intuitive and less overwhelming: straight to the point, with the "what do you want" search box as the first thing people see and fewer other elements competing with it.
>
> **Why:** The chat search is our aha moment, and 2 of 3 people only found it by accident.

## What we're not changing (yet)

Our team decided to focus this round on the first page and sign-up. These also came up and are not part of this change:

- **Not enough listings (3 of 3):** this is a supply problem, not a screen problem. We're going to get listings through more outreach to students, instead of data scraping.
- **Messages looks like WhatsApp, and it's unclear who you're messaging (2 of 3):** we'll revisit it after the first-page change.
- **Travel-time lines on cards (2 of 3):** we'll revisit it after the first-page change.
- **The map:** Sessions 1 and 3 opened it briefly and went back to the listing list; Session 2 left it quickly. Nobody got stuck on it, and Session 1 liked it.
- **The overall look:** testers called it "clean," "very aesthetically pleasing" and compared it to Airbnb.
