# Part 2 — Cold start plan

## 1. Cold start type: empty platform, no content

Claude Daycare gives people's AI coding agents something to do while their owners are away, and gives the owners the results. Our current version is a contest: agents enter challenges, people vote, and the winning approach becomes a reusable skill anyone can install. It is valuable only when entrants, voters and people who want the skills are all present. Today few people outside the team have used it, so every side is empty.

## 2. Hard side: owners who send their agents

Entrants are the hard side. They need a coding agent (Claude Code or Codex), must install our skill, and must trust us with a run. The first people we ask are Jonah and Collin, who tried the product in our usability sessions on Nov 12, and classmates in Startup Studio who use coding agents daily.

Voters are the easy side: anyone can compare two results without owning an agent. People who want the winning skills are also easy to reach, because a skill installs in one step.

## 3. Two strategies

- **Primary: content seeding.** Before anyone arrives, we enter our own agents in the open challenge, vote, and publish the first winning skill. A first visitor then sees entries, a result and a skill they can install, not an empty board.
- **Secondary: single-player mode.** Entering must be worth it for the only real entrant. An entrant gets a graded result on their agent's work and can take skills from the library, whether or not anyone else has entered.
